# Table Colors

**Table colors** decide how four parts of your table look: the **search bar**, the **header row**, the **body rows** (including hover and zebra stripes) and the **pagination bar**. Pick a ready-made scheme and the whole table is recolored in one click, or set each color yourself to match your brand exactly.

- **Preset** gives you 14 ready-made schemes. The **Default** scheme is free; the other 13 are Pro.
- **Custom** (Pro) lets you set every color of every part, one by one.

1. Go to **Ninja Tables** › **Tables** and open a table.
2. Click the **Appearance** tab.
3. In the settings panel, click the **Colors** tab. It is the tab the panel opens on.

![Colors tab in Preset mode with the scheme swatches](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/1.-Preset-Scheme-Swatches.webp)

At the top of the tab, **Color Mode** has two choices: **Preset** and **Custom** (Pro). Every change shows in the live preview straight away. Click **Save** at the top of the editor to keep it.

## Preset: ready-made color schemes

With **Preset** selected, the panel says "Pick a ready-made color scheme." and shows a grid of swatches. Each swatch is a tiny table drawn in that scheme's colors, with a header bar and rows, and its name underneath. Click one to apply it. The selected scheme gets a check mark.

A scheme recolors the whole table at once: the search bar, the header, the rows, the row under the pointer and the pagination bar. You don't have to set anything else.

The 14 schemes are:

**Default**, **White**, **Red**, **Orange**, **Yellow**, **Olive**, **Green**, **Teal**, **Blue**, **Violet**, **Purple**, **Pink**, **Grey** and **Black**.

- **Default** adds no colors of its own. The table follows your theme and the style library you chose in [Rows & Borders](/table-rows-borders). Pick it when your theme already looks the way you want. It is the scheme every new table starts with, and the only one on the free version.
- **The other 13 schemes** (Pro) give you a complete look with one click. Use them when you want a colored header and matching rows without choosing individual colors.

![Preset color schemes with the Blue scheme selected and the table preview in blue](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/2.-Preset-Color-Schemes-With-The-Blue.webp)

## Custom: set each color yourself (Pro)

Select **Custom** when a ready-made scheme is close but not exact, or when you need your brand colors. The panel says "Set each color below. Switching back to Preset uses the scheme instead."

Custom colors are split into four sections, one for each part of the table: **Search Bar Colors**, **Table Header Colors**, **Table Body Colors** and **Pagination Colors**.

- **One section open at a time.** Click a section's heading to open it. Opening another one closes the first.
- **Your edits are kept.** Closing a section does not discard the colors you set in it.
- **Every field starts as Default.** A field that shows **Default** is empty, so that part keeps its normal color from your theme and style library. Set only the colors you want to change.
- **Custom turns on by itself.** The moment you change any color, the table switches to **Custom** mode, so your color takes effect without you touching the mode switch.
- **Back to Preset.** Choose **Preset** again and the table uses the scheme. The colors you typed are not applied while **Preset** is selected.

### Search Bar Colors

The search bar is the strip above the table where visitors type to find a row. Turn it on or off, and change its texts, in [Search Box](/add-or-remove-search-or-filter-functionality).

![Search Bar Colors section with Background, Icon and Border fields](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/3.-Search-Bar-Colors.webp)

- **Background**: the fill color of the search bar.
- **Icon**: the color of the search icon and its button.
- **Border**: the outline around the search bar. Leave it as **Default** for no visible border.

These are the same colors as **Colors** in the **Search** section of the Features tab, so a change in one shows in the other.

### Table Header Colors

The header row holds your column titles. Color it to make the titles stand out from the data below.

![Table Header Colors section with Background, Text and Border fields](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/4.-Table-Header-Colors.webp)

- **Background**: the fill color behind the column titles.
- **Text**: the color of the column titles. Pick a color that contrasts with the background so the titles stay easy to read.
- **Border**: the color of the lines around the header cells.

If the header row is hidden, these colors have nothing to paint. Show it with **Show Header Row** under **Styling** › **Title & Header** (see [Title, Caption & Description](/table-title-description-settings)).

### Table Body Colors

This section colors the rows of data. It has more options than the others, so it is worth reading in full.

![Table Body Colors section with the Default and Hover switch and the alternate row colors](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/5.-Table-Body-Colors-Alternate-Rows.webp)

**Default and Hover.** At the top right of the section, a **Default** / **Hover** switch chooses which state you are coloring.

- **Default**, titled **Rows**: how every row looks normally. Set the **Background**, the **Text** color and the **Border**.
- **Hover**, titled **Rows On Hover**: how the row under the visitor's pointer looks. It has the same three colors. A hover color helps readers follow a row across a wide table.

**Alternate rows (zebra stripes).** **Use Alternate Color Schema For Table Rows** is off by default. Turn it on to give odd and even rows different colors, which makes long tables easier to scan. Two groups appear:

- **Odd Row Colors**: the 1st, 3rd, 5th row and so on. Set a **Background**, a **Text** color and a **Hover Background**.
- **Even Row Colors**: the 2nd, 4th, 6th row and so on. It has the same three colors.

While alternate colors are on, they take the place of the plain **Rows** colors for the body rows. Use the **Rows** colors when every row should look the same, and alternate colors when you want stripes.

::: tip
A **Border** color only shows when the table has borders. Turn on **Bordered Table** under **Styling** › [Rows & Borders](/table-rows-borders).
:::

### Pagination Colors

The pagination bar sits under the table and holds the page numbers. Turn pagination on or off, and set rows per page, in [Pagination](/pagination).

![Pagination Colors section with Background, Active Page and Border fields](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/6.-Pagination-Colors.webp)

- **Background**: the fill color of the pagination bar.
- **Active Page**: the color that marks the page the visitor is on. Pick something that stands out from the background.
- **Border**: the color of the lines around the pagination buttons.

These are the same colors as **Colors** in the **Pagination** section of the Features tab, so a change in one shows in the other.

## Pick a color

Every color field works the same way. A small swatch sits next to a text box. A checkered swatch with the word **Default** means the color is not set.

![Color picker open with the preset color row, the transparency slider and the hex box](/images/styling-design/table-color-settings/7.-Color-Picker-Popup.webp)

- **Click the swatch** to open the picker. Choose from the ready-made colors, or drag in the color area and along the color strip to mix your own. Drag the transparency slider to make a color see-through. Click **OK** to apply the color, or **Clear** to go back to **Default**.
- **Or type a value** in the text box: a hex code such as `#14b8a6`, a short hex such as `#fff`, a hex with transparency such as `#14b8a680`, or `rgb()` or `rgba()`.
- **Opaque colors** are saved as a normal hex code. A color with transparency is saved as `rgba()`.
- **Clear a color** with **Clear** in the picker, or with the small **x** button that appears next to a set color. The field goes back to **Default**.

## What the free version does

On the free version, **Default** is the only scheme. Clicking another scheme, **Custom** or a color field opens the upgrade popup and changes nothing. If a table still holds paid colors, they are reset to the **Default** scheme when you save.

Click **Save** to keep your colors.

## Common questions

::: faq
== I set a border color but nothing changed.
A border color only shows when the table has borders. Turn on **Bordered Table** under **Styling** › [Rows & Borders](/table-rows-borders), then check the color again.
== My custom colors don't show on my site.
Check three things. **Custom** must be selected at the top of the Colors tab, because custom colors are applied only in **Custom** mode. You must have clicked **Save**. And Custom colors need Pro: on the free version a color field opens the upgrade popup and changes nothing.
== What happens to my custom colors when I click Preset?
While **Preset** is selected the table uses the scheme, and your custom colors are not applied. Select **Custom** again to apply them.
== Why can't I change the color of one row or one cell?
Table colors apply to the whole table. To color a single row or cell, use [Row & Cell Colors](/tables-row-color). To color cells by their value, use [Conditional Formatting](/conditional-formatting).
== Do the pagination and search colors change anywhere else?
They are the same colors you find in the **Search** and **Pagination** sections of the Features tab. Change them in either place.
:::
